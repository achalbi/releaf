What this version is and isn't

v4 is the union of v2's "Scan-as-hero with pre-tag categories" and v3's "6 large tiles for the rest of the modes." It doesn't choose between them — it stacks them vertically and lets the user scroll. That's a real design decision: the sheet becomes the one place the user goes for any kind of capture, with Scan-with-pre-tag occupying the first viewport and everything else one swipe down.

Search and calendar — moved

v3 had search and calendar icon buttons in the sheet header (right of the "Capture" title). v4 doesn't have a sheet title anymore — it has "What arrived?" instead, and the layout doesn't accommodate buttons next to it. Search and calendar move back to the Home header (the TUESDAY · April 28 row that's already showing them above the scrim). That's where they live in the existing app today; v4 just leaves them there.

Where the sheet fails (worth pre-empting)

It's tall. Total content is ~1100 px on a 390-px-wide phone. The sheet shows ~700 px above the safe area. So the user always has to scroll if they want anything from the v3 grid. That's a deliberate cost: it makes Scan-with-pre-tag the path of least resistance, at the price of one extra swipe for everything else.

It conflates two surfaces. v2 was a scan-first capture sheet. v3 was an everything-equal capture sheet. v4 says "scan-first and everything-equal" which is coherent but heavy. If most users just want Notes or Voice, this design is asking them to scroll past Scan every single time.

Alternative if scroll-fatigue shows up: swap the Day/Recents toggle for a Scan / Capture toggle. Scan tab = the v2 top half. Capture tab = the v3 grid. Same sheet, two contents, no scroll. Worth keeping in mind if users complain.

Open questions

1 · Is the scroll cost acceptable?
The scan workflow gets the prime real estate. Notes / Photo / Voice users pay one swipe. Recommend: instrument top-of-scroll vs scrolled tap rates after launch — if >70% of taps land on the v3 grid, switch to the Scan/Capture tab alternative above.
2 · Should Day/Recents stick during scroll?
Phone B shows the Day/Recents toggle still visible at the top of the sheet even after scrolling. The implementation can either stick it (sticky-positioned within the sheet body) or scroll it away. Recommend: stick it — Recents is a "show me what just happened" affordance and shouldn't require scrolling back to the top to reach.
3 · "What arrived?" — does it scroll out or stay?
In phone B I let it scroll out. Big serif headers as sticky elements would overstay their welcome and crowd the actual interactive content. Recommend: let it scroll out; the Day/Recents toggle is the persistent context.
4 · How does the v3 grid behave when Day/Recents is on Recents?
Recents shows a list of recent captures, not a capture form. The v3 grid is irrelevant when Recents is selected. Decision: Recents tab replaces the entire scrollable content (Scan hero + pre-tags + divider + v3 grid) with a list of last ~15 captures. The Day/Recents toggle is a true content swap, not an overlay.

Implementation footprint

One file changes meaningfully on each platform. Android — QuickCaptureSheet.kt: convert the sheet body to a LazyColumn with three sections — Scan hero composable, pre-tag LazyVerticalGrid, and the v3 LazyVerticalGrid of capture tiles — plus the divider row. SheetState stays at skipPartiallyExpanded = true so the sheet always opens to its largest stop. iOS — QuickCaptureSheet.swift mirrors with a single ScrollView hosting the same three sections. Tokens: keep the v2 mustard ramp; the v3 tile washes already exist. CaptureMode.kt matches v3 (Notes · Photo · Voice · Todo · Contact · Pin). No SQL migration; pre-tag categories ride existing tags table.
