Where ~250 px came from

v4 stacked everything vertically and let the user scroll. v5 keeps every section but tightens each one. The biggest single cut is the pre-tag grid → chip row, which gives back a full row of vertical real estate while preserving the "tap a category, scan into it" interaction. Everything else is dimensional trim.

Three real cuts

1 · Pre-tag grid → horizontal-scroll chip row (saves ~124 px). Five most-frequent tags + "New tag" sit in a single horizontal row. User swipes left to reveal more. Same icon + name + count info; about half the row is visible at rest, the rest peeks past the right edge as a discoverability cue. Tapping a chip pre-tags the next scan exactly like before.

2 · Compact Scan hero (saves ~70 px). Same gradient, same paper detail in the corner, same buttons — just less padding around the title and tighter line-height. The "Today's first capture" eyebrow is shorter; the "Scan a page" title drops from 30 px to 22 px serif; the blurb stays two lines but shorter copy ("Auto-crops, OCRs, files under today" instead of "Auto-crops, OCRs, and files under today"). All visual signature retained.

3 · Title block tightens (saves ~34 px). "What arrived?" drops from 36 px to 30 px serif. Date moves from a separate italic subtitle line to a small meta label aligned with the Day/Recents toggle on the right. The italic subtitle below "What arrived?" becomes shorter ("pick a category to pre-tag your scan") since the date is now elsewhere.

What didn't get cut

The Day/Recents toggle, "What arrived?" header voice, the mustard hero with paper + crop-corner brackets, the pre-tag affordance with active-amber state, the "or capture differently" divider, all six capture tiles with distinct color washes, and the "Hold the Leaf button" footer all survive intact. Nothing was dropped — everything was resized.

Consequences worth knowing

1 · The pre-tag chip row hides tags past the visible edge
In a grid, all 6 tags are visible at once. In a horizontal scroll, the user only sees ~3 at rest. Net cost: tags 4–6 are slightly less discoverable. Mitigation: rank by recency-weighted frequency (already proposed in v2) so the top 3 are the user's most-used; the "scroll for more →" hint signals the rest is reachable.
2 · Capture tiles got a hair smaller
Aspect ratio 1.05:1 instead of 1:1. Icon chip 32 px instead of 38 px. Hint text 9.5 px instead of 11 px. Still well above 44 dp tap-target minimums for the whole tile, but the visual presence dropped. Watch: if usability testing shows users miss "Pin" or "Contact" labels, bump back to 1:1 and let the sheet scroll a tiny bit.
3 · "What arrived?" shrunk — does it still feel editorial?
30 px serif is still distinct from any other text on the screen. The voice survives. If it doesn't: drop "What arrived?" entirely and let the Scan hero's "Scan a page" title carry the framing. Saves another ~50 px and gives the sheet more breathing room.
4 · iPhone SE / smaller phones
v5's height budget assumes a 390 × 844 phone. iPhone SE is 375 × 667 — that's ~70 px less vertical room. v5 will need scroll there, or the sheet starts higher (top=40 instead of top=78), or the alternative Scan | Capture tab swap from v4's open question takes over.

Implementation footprint

Same files as v4, smaller dimensions. Android — QuickCaptureSheet.kt: pre-tag grid becomes a LazyRow instead of LazyVerticalGrid; everything else just gets smaller padding / font-size constants. iOS mirrors with ScrollView(.horizontal) for the pre-tag chips. The compose SheetState stays at skipPartiallyExpanded = true but the natural content height now fits well below the largest stop, so the sheet sits at its content size instead of maxing out.
